Your Case Load

Organizational Groups in Virtuous

Your case load is stored in Virtuous as an Organizational Group, a saved list of the contacts and individuals you are personally developing a relationship with. "Case load" and "Organizational Group" mean the same thing: the group is the Virtuous term, the case load is what we call it day to day.

That same group is what you point a query at when you want to email everyone on your case load at once. Emailing Your Case Load walks through it end to end.

Four automatic workflows run against that group. Each one checks your case load on a schedule, and when a contact matches, it creates a task assigned to you. Nothing on this page is something you create by hand.

The task appears right away in the TASKS panel on your Virtuous dashboard. The email comes later: Virtuous sends a reminder 48 hours before a task is due, and if several are coming due at once they arrive together in one email.

Because every workflow is scoped to your group, every notification you receive is about one of your own partners.

Case loads are set up per worker/volunteer, and the current list is kept internally rather than published here. If you are not sure whether you have a case load set up, or whether these workflows are running for you, just ask Mark.

The Notifications

Trigger, exact wording, and due date for each

Birthday

A partner's birthday is a week out. Your cue to send a card or make a call in time for the day.

Runs
Daily, against your case load
Fires when
An individual on your case load has a birthday exactly one week from today
Task title
Birthday
Task details
This person's birthday is upcoming
Due in
6 days, which lands the day before the birthday
Task type
General

Watch for: this workflow reads the birthdate on the individual record, not the contact. If a partner's birthday has never been entered in Virtuous, the workflow has nothing to match and no task will ever fire for them. In a household, each spouse needs their own birthdate filled in to get their own task.

Thank You

Someone on your case load just gave. Reach out and thank them.

Runs
Daily, against your case load
Fires when
A contact on your case load gave a new gift yesterday
Task title
Thank You
Task details
One of you contacts gave recently. Reach out and let them know we are thankful.
Due in
7 days
Task type
General

Watch for: the scan looks at yesterday's gifts, so a gift given today reaches you tomorrow. If you do not work with giving data directly, this task is how you find out that a partner has donated.

Prelapsed Donor Alert

A partner is drifting. An early warning so you can re-engage before they lapse for good.

Runs
Daily, against your case load
Fires when
A contact on your case load has not given within the past 6 months
Task title
Alert -recent no giving (6 months)
Task details
This contact has not given within 6 months and tagged at "prelapsed"
Due in
7 days
Task type
General

Watch for: this is aimed at infrequent donor partners, the ones who give occasionally rather than on a recurring schedule, so a quiet stretch is a real signal. Recurring donors are covered by the Past Due workflow below instead. Contacts matched here also carry the Prelapsed Donor tag.

Contact with Past Due Recurring Gifts

A recurring partner's expected payment did not come through. Follow up and get the schedule back on track.

Runs
Daily, against your case load
Fires when
A contact on your case load has a recurring gift whose status is Past Due
Task title
Follow up on Past Due Recurring Gift
Task details
Check the attached Contact record's recurring gift schedule for missed payment details and reach out to update payment information and/or connect with donor.
Due in
5 days
Past Due means
One or more expected recurring gift payments have been missed

Watch for: Virtuous applies a 30-day grace period after a missed payment before it changes the status to Past Due, so the task reaches you about a month after the payment was actually missed. Open the contact record and check the recurring gift schedule to see which payment was missed before you reach out. Most cases are an expired or replaced card rather than a decision to stop giving.
